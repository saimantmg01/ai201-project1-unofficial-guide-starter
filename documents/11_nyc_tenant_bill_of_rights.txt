TITLE: New York City Tenant Bill of Rights
SOURCE TYPE: Official NYC Housing Preservation and Development webpage
URL: https://www.nyc.gov/site/hpd/services-and-information/tenant-bill-of-rights.page
RETRIEVED: 2026-06-08

NYC tenants have rights involving fair access to housing, housing quality,
leases and security deposits, eviction protections, and freedom from landlord
harassment.

Housing must be safe and maintained, including working locks, smoke and carbon
monoxide detectors, adequate common-area lighting, essential services, and
conditions free of mold, leaks, and garbage.

Landlords may not charge more than $20 for an apartment application. A security
deposit may not exceed one month's rent and generally must be returned within
14 days after the tenant leaves, subject to lawful deductions.

A landlord cannot evict a tenant without going to court and obtaining the
required judgment and warrant. A landlord also cannot force a tenant out by
changing locks, withholding a key, removing belongings, cutting utilities, or
using harassment.

Tenants may organize a tenant association and report violations to 311 without
retaliation. For help, tenants can call 311 and request the Tenant Helpline.

RELIABILITY NOTE: This is a general educational summary, not legal advice.
